Magical Ability / Power:

Blood Magic.

How did they acquire it?

Research into blood drove him deeper into a cult study. That study led him to an entity that exists and is connected to the blood. That creature is what he has gained his power from. By understanding the blood, by understanding the rules of this being, his power grows.

What does their magic look or feel like?

It’s blood bruh. Warm or hot depending on the spell, especially powerful spell can have the vitae sizzling on impact or chewing through their target like acid.

Does their power have a cost, weakness, or limitation?

Using his own blood exhausts him and may force him to rest. His power is limited by his understanding of the creature that lives within Vitae.

BACKSTORY

Youngest son of the noble T’shaal family. First memories of my older brother–sickly, pale, with pustules weeping from his skin. He was the one destined to take over our noble line. Thirdborn, I was. My oldest brother, I still remember to this day.Frail, laying in bed, boils on his skin, weeping pus into the mattress. Mother telling me to hug him, give him a kiss on the forehead before bed at night. I felt sympathy, but more importantly, I felt disgust. The idea of touching him, his skin pale, half rotten, peeling off of him like tissue paper. The thought in my young mind, all I wanted was to heal him, make him better.

It's incredible how things we think of children as children end up translating to us as adults. I thought I would become a doctor, heal the world's wrongs, one body at a time. And so I strived day after day, until I finally was there, studying among the great and the good.

That it seemed I wasn't quite good enough. I failed, fell away, like many do. My brother passed away that summer. He would have died before I'd ever even finished school. My reason was gone. And with that, I had nothing shackling me or giving me pause. I'd spent my whole life studying the body, the blood. I couldn't manage it. The idea of leaving the city, returning home, and having wasted everything for nothing, and so if I could not heal him, then I would heal the world instead. It made sense to me at the time, though. Having failed already, I apprenticed myself at a local surgery, a small place in the city. They let me scribe at night, stay there late, clean, and the owner was kind enough to allow me to make notes on the cadavers that would pass through.

Of course, you can only do so much with cadavers. A small surgery like that often needed to have deals and obviously understandings with different parts and elements of the city in order to continue to function. It's the way of things. And over time, I became somewhat trusted. Not as a doctor in my own right. No, no, never. But a pair of hands, someone to check on overnight patients, someone trusted.

We had a deal with the local prison. Perhaps one injured in a brawl in a tavern or one sat there for quite a long while had come across some injuring one inmate had done to another one night I had the thought… Live subjects are so rare and who would miss this person? I remember not his face. Some murderer, some degenerate. He didn't get better come the next morning. And then after a while, they had a habit of never quite getting better. And so I continued my research. I knew it was in the blood. It was something within that. I could feel it calling to me. Almost an itch in the back of my head. Ever since I first touched my brother when I was so young. Ever since I saw the weeping bloody pustules coming from his body, I felt something there pulling me, calling me. And the deeper I dug, the more I knew it existed until I found it.

One night in the surgery room, my hands caked in blood and bile and who knows whatever else. I felt it. Something whispering within my mind. Something not entirely me. I followed it deeper and deeper. The blood was always the answer. I just needed to find the right question. Unfortunately, it seems that one of the prisoners I had experimented on was the son of a rather influential businessman, who in turn had raised the issue to one of the city's governors, who in turn had volunteered the information to the city guard.

It seemed as if my luck had turned. And then I saw it. A boat to new horizons, a promise of adventure, and perhaps the chance to heal the world's ills. I know when to leave a party before it starts to go downhill. And something told me that this party was just about to be spoiled.
